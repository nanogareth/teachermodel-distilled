**Everyone watches the same delivery and scores it alone.** Two recorded excerpts are played once
each, with no pausing. After each excerpt every participant scores all five domains in silence: a
whole number, the anchor it rests on, and the moment on the recording that shows it. Only then does
anyone speak.

**Then the room meets the reference.** You take a show of hands for each domain onto the
flipchart, read out the reference score, and every participant writes it beside their own in red.
The discussion starts with the domains where the room was furthest from the reference, and it is
about anchors and timestamps, never about who scored best.

## BEFORE

### What you need in your head

- **Why scoring alike matters.** Clearance rests on observers' rubric scores. If two observers
  watch one delivery and score it differently, a participant's clearance depends on who turned up
  to observe. Calibration (W5) holds observers to 90% of their scores within one point of the
  reference and 60% exact (`standards/rules.yaml`, `calibration`). Today participants practise the
  same skill from the other side of the form, so that they know what their observers listen for.
- **This is not a calibration.** Calibration is a W5 exercise for observers, scored against a
  private reference through the scoring pages, and it decides whether an observer may observe.
  Today decides nothing. The scores are formative, they are not observations, they never count
  toward clearance, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005).
- **A score rests on an anchor and a timestamp.** Every score on the form has two things beside it
  in the evidence note: the words of the anchor it rests on, and the time on the recording's clock
  when the moment came, for example `14:10`. A score without both cannot be discussed, because
  there is nothing to replay and no wording to compare.
- **"Not seen" versus 1, and why today has no "not seen".** In session 1, a domain with no moment
  in the slice was "not seen", and a moment that came and was missed scored, usually 1. The
  reference covers all five domains, and the head of training cut each excerpt so that every domain
  has a moment. So today every domain gets a whole number. Where a domain's moment is thin, for
  example one remote learner on the call and one question that went only to the room, the
  participant scores what is there and writes its timestamp. The reference made the same call from
  the same kind of moment, and you read that call out at the reveal.
- **The first score is the evidence.** Scores are written in black pen before anyone speaks. At the
  end of each silent scoring you take every black pen and hand out red ones, so anything written
  after that is visibly an annotation. A participant may disagree, explain and annotate in red, and
  the black number stands. The coordinator types only the black numbers.
- **You read the reference; you do not defend it.** The head of training set the reference scores
  and wrote a note for each: the anchor, the timestamps, and the call made where a moment was thin.
  The anchors are still draft wording, to be refined through calibration (`standards/rubric.yaml`).
  If a participant makes a case against the reference with a timestamp, write the domain and the
  timestamp on your capture list for the head of training. Do not change the reference in the room.
- **Agreement is private; scores are not.** Scores are shown by hands, as they were said aloud in
  session 1. Each participant's agreement with the reference, the count of exact and within-one
  scores, stays on their own form. The flipchart holds tally marks and no names. Never ask who
  matched most often, never rank, and stop anyone who offers their total to the room.
- **The people in the recordings are real.** They are instructors filmed during real deliveries,
  and their learners may be seen or heard. The excerpts are shown only in this room, from
  your laptop. They are never sent, never put on a shared drive or a link, and never played from a
  participant's device. Only the training function holds them. Nobody names the instructor, even
  if they recognise them. Laptops stay closed and phones face down while an excerpt plays, and
  nobody photographs the screen.
- **Nothing about the recordings leaves the room.** The forms and notes sheets hold timestamps and
  reference scores. The recordings are used again for later groups, and possibly for calibration,
  so a sheet taken home would hand the next person the answers. You collect every sheet at the
  wrap. Participants keep their pack, which holds no reference scores.
- **You may not know the subject.** Score technical depth, as in session 1, on what anyone can
  see: a question that got an answer, an "I would test it by...", or a question that was deflected.
  The reference note tells you what the reference saw.

### The recordings and the reference sheet

The design names three reference-scored recordings, the same three the calibration phase gate
needs. Session 5 plays two of them, labelled `ref-a` and `ref-b`. The third, `ref-c`, is the spare
for a group in which someone appears in `ref-a` or `ref-b`. The labels name the same recording for
every group, so the comparison file in the private store also says which recordings each
participant has seen.

The head of training gives you, by the route they use for private-store material:

- **The two excerpt files.** `ref-a` is 20 minutes and `ref-b` 15, each cut from a longer delivery
  so that every domain has a moment. Each file starts at `00:00`, and the reference timestamps are
  in that clock. Delete your copies after the session, and tell the head of training you have.
- **The reference sheet,** printed, one page per recording. For each domain: the reference score,
  the anchor words it rests on, the timestamps that show it, and one or two sentences on what the
  reference saw there. It stays in your hands all session and goes back to the head of training
  afterwards. Participants never see it, and you never read it out whole: you read one domain's
  note at a time, at the reveal.

The reference also exists as a YAML file in the private store, in the same shape as a
participant's scores file. You do not need it. The coordinator uses it after the session, and it
never goes to you or to a participant as a file.

**Open question for the head of training.** Whether these recordings are also W5 calibration
exercise recordings is not decided (`DESIGN.md`, "Left open"). If they are, a participant will
later calibrate on a recording they have already discussed with its reference. Two things in this
pack limit that: every sheet with a reference score on it is collected, and the comparison file
records which recordings each person scored, so a W5 exercise can give them one they have not seen.

### What each anchor looks like on a recording

The anchor wording is on the form. This table says where in a recorded excerpt the evidence for
each domain tends to show, so that you can point participants at the moment rather than the
person.

| Domain | Look and listen for |
|---|---|
| Technical depth | Each learner question you can hear. Was it answered, answered with "I would test it by...", or deflected? Did an unsure answer get followed up? |
| Prior knowledge | The first minutes of a topic. Did the instructor find out what the room knew, say what it showed, and change what came next? |
| Conceptual questioning | Every question the instructor asked. Recall, rhetorical, or one whose wrong answers point to a misconception? Was there a vote, a discussion between learners, a revote? |
| Lab facilitation | Anything on screen or in the room that failed: a learner stuck, an error, a demo that broke. What happened to everyone else while it was handled? |
| Remote and inclusive | The remote learners on the call. Did they get the same questions, checks and help as the room, and when: during an activity or only at its end? |

### 0:33-0:51 Phase 2 -- tally, reveal and discuss recording A (plenary)

**0:33-0:35 The tally (step 3).** For each domain in form order: *"Technical depth. Hands up if you
scored 1... 2... 3... 4."* Put a tally mark in the grid for each hand. Five domains, about twenty
seconds each. Count the marks against the number of people in the room after each row.

**0:35-0:37 The reveal (step 4).** For each domain, say the reference score from your reference
sheet and write it in the Reference column: *"Technical depth: the reference scored 3."* Say the
number and nothing else yet. Participants write it in red beside their score, with the difference,
for example "ref 3, -2". Then circle on the grid every tally mark two or more points from the
reference.

**0:37-0:49 The discussion (step 5).** Take the domains in this order: the most circled marks
first, then the most marks one point away. Three domains fit, at about four minutes each. A domain
where everyone matched the reference is not discussed. Read its note in one sentence at 0:48 if
there is time.

**Each domain, about four minutes, in this order:**

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A volunteer furthest from the reference | 0:45 | Their black score, in the stem, with its timestamp. Ask the room for the furthest score on the grid, for example *"Who scored 1 here and will say why?"* Nobody has to answer |
| 2 | A volunteer at the reference | 0:45 | The same stem, with their timestamp |
| 3 | You | 1:00 | The reference note for this domain, read from the sheet: the score, the anchor's words, the timestamps, and what the reference saw. For a thin moment, read the call it made |
| 4 | You | up to 1:00 | Replay a moment, only when turns 1 and 2 named different timestamps. Play from ten seconds before the earlier one, at most a minute, at most twice in this phase |
| 5 | You | 0:30 | The words in the anchor that decide between the two scores |

An invented example of turns 3 and 5. Read the real note from your reference sheet, not this one.

*"Conceptual questioning. The reference scored 3. The anchor for 3 says 'uses at least one question
whose wrong answers map to known misconceptions, and changes course on the result'. At 14:10 the
instructor put a four-option question on the screen. At 15:30, after the hands, they said that half

reference did not give 4, because there was no discussion between learners and no revote. If you
gave 1, look at your timestamp. The questions at 03:00 were recall, and they would score 1 on their
own. The anchor asks for at least one question, so the one at 14:10 is enough for 3."*

**Then turn 5, in your own words:** *"So the words that decide it are 'at least one question'. One
good question in twenty minutes is a 3, even when the rest are recall."*

  comparison with calibration, and here it is: 'for comparison only: calibration (W5) asks for 90%
  within one point and 60% exact; this is not a calibration'. The recordings and the reference go
  back to the head of training today."*
- **1:30** End.

### Recovery moves

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| **The excerpt will not play, or has no sound** (T-15 should have caught it) | Try the other player and the other speakers once, up to three minutes. If it still fails, the session cannot run: there is no other copy you may use. Never play it from a participant's laptop, a link or a streaming service. Rebook with the coordinator. |
| **A participant arrives up to five minutes late** | They take a seat, close their laptop, and score everything from what they saw, with "joined at 04:30" (the recording's time) written at the top of the form. The scores stand. |
| **A participant arrives more than five minutes late** | They can stay and watch, and they do not score that recording. The record needs two scored recordings, so they take session 5 with a later group. |
| **A participant says they know the instructor in the recording** | Quietly, at the next gate-check: they keep scoring, and they do not name the person. If they are themselves in the recording, stop scoring them on it at once; they take session 5 with a later group, and the head of training is told so that the T-7 check catches it next time. |
| **Someone speaks during an excerpt or a silent scoring** | Point at the rules card. Do not pause. If a score was said aloud, the speaker's form is still typed as written; note it on your sheet. |
| **A participant writes "not seen" or a range** | During the scoring, point at the box and the rules card. They write a whole number before the tally. After the tally it cannot be added: the coordinator cannot type that domain, the check refuses the file, and the participant takes session 5 again with a later group. |
| **A participant wants to change a score after the reveal** | They write the new score in red with the reason. The black score stands, and the coordinator types the black score. |
| **A participant writes in black after the reveal** (a spare pen) | Ask them to mark it in red as written after the reveal. Note the form for the coordinator. |
| **Someone asks how many others matched, or offers their own total** | *"Your count stays on your form. We compare anchors here, not people."* |
| **A participant asks for a copy of the recording or of the reference** | No. Both stay with the training function. They may write down anchor wording from the form, which is on every blank form anyway. |
| **The discussion on one domain stalls** | At five minutes, stop it, note it for the capture list, and go to the next domain. |
| **You are 3+ minutes late at 0:33** | Take the time from the share-out (down to two minutes), then the reflection, then the Phase 4 discussion. Never shorten an excerpt or a silent scoring. |
| **Someone asks whether the scores count** | No. They are formative, they are not observations, they never count toward clearance, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005). This is not a calibration. |

## AFTER

### Close protocol

- [ ] Count the forms again away from the room: two per participant, each with an Observer id,
      five black scores, and its notes sheet stapled behind it.
- [ ] Hand the forms to the coordinator the same day in a closed folder. They hold reference scores
      and timestamps from recordings of real people: do not photograph them and do not leave them
      in the room.
- [ ] Return the reference sheet and the two tally grids to the head of training the same day. Delete
      the excerpts from your laptop and tell the head of training you have.
- [ ] The coordinator types each participant's black scores into one file, named by instructor id,
      for example `ttt5-0007.yaml`. Only the black numbers: ignore everything in red.

```yaml
recordings:
  - recording: ref-a
    scores: {technical-depth: 3, prior-knowledge: 2, conceptual-questioning: 1, lab-facilitation: 3, remote-inclusive: 2}
  - recording: ref-b
    scores: {technical-depth: 4, prior-knowledge: 3, conceptual-questioning: 3, lab-facilitation: 2, remote-inclusive: 1}
```

- [ ] The coordinator runs it against the reference file in the private store:
      `teachermodel compare-scores <scores file> --reference <reference file> --out <comparison file>`.
      With the scores above and a made-up reference (the numbers are invented and are not the real
      reference), it prints:

```text
ref-a: exact 60%, within one point 80%
  technical-depth          3  reference 3  +0
  prior-knowledge          2  reference 2  +0
  conceptual-questioning   1  reference 3  -2
  lab-facilitation         3  reference 2  +1
  remote-inclusive         2  reference 2  +0

  technical-depth          4  reference 3  +1
  prior-knowledge          3  reference 3  +0

  lab-facilitation         2  reference 2  +0
  remote-inclusive         1  reference 2  -1
for comparison only: calibration (W5) asks for 90% within one point and 60% exact; this is not a calibration

- [ ] If the file is wrong, the command writes nothing, prints one line per problem and exits 1.
      A label problem is a typing error, and the coordinator corrects it from the form header. A
      half score or a missing domain is how the participant wrote it; the coordinator tells you,

```
- [ ] The comparison file is the evidence. It holds the participant's scores and the reference, so
      it stays in the private store. The coordinator stores it under one key, deletes the typed

- [ ] The coordinator checks it with `teachermodel course status --instructor tm:instructor/<id>`:
      session 5 now shows a date instead of "missing".
- [ ] Keep the paper forms until the head of training says what happens to them:
      `standards/retention.yaml` has no entry for training evidence yet.

### If a participant missed the session

They join a later group's session 5. There is no solo route: the recordings are shown only in a
session, and the discussion needs other scorers in the room.

### What participants take forward

- **Every other session.** Session 5 can run at any point. Participants who take it before
  sessions 1 to 4 hear the anchors in their peers' feedback with a recording behind them, and
  participants who take it last see the anchors they were scored on from the observer's side.
- **Being observed.** Box R.1 names the anchor they will listen for in their own teaching.
- **Becoming an observer.** Later in the programme some of them will calibrate as observers (W5),
  against the same 90% and 60%.
