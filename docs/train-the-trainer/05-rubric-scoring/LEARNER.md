## Before the session

### At a glance

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| **Duration** | One 90-minute block, and about fifteen minutes the evening before |
| **Group** | Four to six participants and one trainer |
| **Scored** | Two excerpts, by you, on every domain of the printed form |
| **You keep** | This booklet. Forms and notes are handed in before you leave |
| **Recorded** | Your marks set against the reference, by the coordinator afterwards |
| **Counts toward clearance** | No. It is practice: neither an observation nor a calibration |

### What this session is for

Later in the programme, observers will watch your classes and mark them against the rubric,
and clearance depends on what they write. Two people watching one class ought to agree; otherwise
your result would turn on whoever happened to attend. That is why observers are trained, then
checked against fixed reference marks, before anyone relies on them.

Today you do the observers' work. You watch two recorded deliveries, score them as an observer
would, and then hear what the reference scores were and which anchor each one rests on. Where you
and the reference differ, you find out which words in the anchor made the difference. Those are the
words your own observers will be listening for.

### What you will be able to do

1. Score a recorded delivery on all five domains, each score with the anchor it rests on and the
   moment on the recording that shows it.
2. Say, for any score, which words of the anchor decided it, and point to the moment.
3. Compare your scores with reference scores, and explain a difference from the anchors rather
   than from your impression of the instructor.
4. Name the anchor you will listen for in your own teaching the next time you are observed.

### The recordings

The two recordings, called recording A and recording B, are excerpts of real technical deliveries:
twenty minutes and fifteen minutes. Each was cut so that something happens in every domain, and
each has been scored in advance on all five domains. Those scores are the **reference**.

The instructors in the recordings are real people, filmed while teaching real classes, and their
learners may be seen or heard. So:

- The recordings are shown only in the room, from the trainer's laptop. They are never sent to
  you, and you cannot have a copy.
- Nobody names the instructor, even if you think you know them. If you do, tell the trainer
  quietly at the next one-minute briefing.
- Laptops stay closed and phones face down while a recording plays. Nobody photographs the screen.
- Your forms and notes sheets stay in the room. Later groups watch the same recordings, and a
  sheet with timestamps and reference scores on it would give them the answers.

Nothing about you is recorded today.

### How you score

You know the form, the anchors and the stem from session 1. Three things are different today.

**Every domain gets a score.** In session 1, a domain with no moment in the slice was "not seen",
and a moment that came and was missed was scored, usually 1. Today there is no "not seen": the
excerpts were cut so that every domain has a moment, and the reference scored all five. Where a
domain's moment is thin, score what is there and write the time. For example, if there is one
remote learner on the call and the only question in the excerpt goes to the room, that is a
moment for remote and inclusive delivery, and it gets a score. The reference had to make the same
call.

**Every score rests on an anchor and a timestamp.** In the evidence note under each domain, write
the anchor's words that decided your score and the time on the recording's clock when it showed,
for example `14:10`. The clock is on the screen, counting from `00:00` at the start of the excerpt.
A score must be one whole number from 1 to 4: no halves, no "2-3".

**Black pen before the reveal, red pen after.** You score in black. When the scoring ends, the
trainer swaps your black pen for a red one. After that you can write anything you like, in red,
and the black score stands. Your first score is the evidence. If you change your mind in the
discussion, write the new score and your reason in red beside it.

### Where to look in a recording

The anchor wording is on the form. This table says where in an excerpt each domain tends to show.

| Domain | Look and listen for |
|---|---|
| Technical depth | Each learner question you can hear. Was it answered, answered with "I would test it by...", or deflected? Did an unsure answer get followed up? |
| Prior knowledge | The first minutes of a topic. Did the instructor find out what the room knew, say what it showed, and change what came next? |
| Conceptual questioning | Every question the instructor asked. Recall, rhetorical, or one whose wrong answers point to a misconception? Was there a vote, a discussion between learners, a revote? |
| Lab facilitation | Anything that failed: a learner stuck, an error on screen, a demo that broke. What happened to everyone else while it was handled? |
| Remote and inclusive | The remote learners on the call. Did they get the same questions, checks and help as the room, and when: during an activity, or only at its end? |

### How to say a score

The stem is the session 1 stem, with the time added:

> "I scored [domain] at [number]. The anchor for [number] says [its words]. What I saw was [what
> happened], at [time on the recording]."

Two invented examples, from a recording you will not see:

> "I scored prior knowledge at 2. The anchor for 2 says 'runs the pre-check but does not act on
> it'. At 01:40 the instructor asked for hands on who had used the library before, most hands went
> up, and at 02:30 they started the worked example from the beginning anyway."

> "I scored lab facilitation at 3. The anchor for 3 says 'diagnoses a failing environment or
> non-deterministic output in front of the room and keeps the cohort moving'. At 11:05 a learner's
> install failed. The instructor told the room it was the proxy, sent everyone else on to the next
> exercise, and fixed it at that laptop."

## How the session runs

One continuous 90-minute block in one room. Before each phase the trainer gives a one-minute
briefing to the whole room.

| When | What runs |
|------|-----------|
| 0:00-0:06 | Welcome: why scoring alike matters, and the rules for today |
| 0:06-0:07 | Briefing: recording A |
| 0:07-0:32 | **Phase 1**: watch recording A (20 min), then score it alone in silence (5 min) |
| 0:32-0:33 | Briefing: the reveal |
| 0:33-0:51 | **Phase 2**: the show of hands, the reference scores, and the discussion of recording A |
| 0:51-0:52 | Briefing: recording B |
| 0:52-1:11 | **Phase 3**: watch recording B (15 min), then score it alone in silence (4 min) |
| 1:11-1:12 | Briefing: the second reveal |
| 1:12-1:22 | **Phase 4**: the show of hands, the reference scores, and the discussion of recording B |
| 1:22-1:26 | Share-out: the anchor that moved you |
| 1:26-1:28 | Reflection, written |
| 1:28-1:30 | Wrap: the trainer collects every form and notes sheet |

### The rules for the room

The rules in "The recordings" and "How you score" above are on a card on the wall. One more goes
with them: score alone. Nobody speaks from the first second of a recording until the show of hands.

### The reveal

After each recording, the trainer takes a show of hands for each domain ("hands up if you scored
1... 2...") and marks them on the flipchart, with no names. Then the trainer reads out the
reference score for each domain, and you write it in red beside your own, with the difference: for
example "ref 3, -2" beside a 1.

The discussion starts with the domain where the room was furthest from the reference. Someone who
scored far from it says their score in the stem, with the time. Someone who matched it does the
same. The trainer reads the reference's note for that domain: the anchor it rests on, the moments
it rests on, and what the reference saw there. The trainer may replay a moment, a minute at most.
Then the trainer names the words in the anchor that decide between the two scores. Nobody has to
volunteer, and nobody changes a black score.

### Your count

At the end of each reveal you count, on your own form, the domains where your score equals the
reference and the domains within one point of it. Calibration, the check observers take later,
asks for 90% within one point and 60% exact. That is there for comparison only: today is not a
calibration.

Your count is yours. It is never put on the flipchart, never compared with anyone else's, and
nobody is asked for it. You do not need to say it in the share-out.

## After the session

### Your record

The trainer collects both forms and both notes sheets at 1:28 and gives them to the coordinator the
same day. The coordinator types the black score from each domain on each form into a short file,
like this one, which holds invented scores:

```yaml
recordings:
  - recording: ref-a
    scores: {technical-depth: 3, prior-knowledge: 2, conceptual-questioning: 1, lab-facilitation: 3, remote-inclusive: 2}
  - recording: ref-b
    scores: {technical-depth: 4, prior-knowledge: 3, conceptual-questioning: 3, lab-facilitation: 2, remote-inclusive: 1}
```

Only the black numbers are typed; nothing in red is. The coordinator then runs a command that
compares the file with the reference, domain by domain, and stores the comparison in the private
store as your session 5 record, dated the day of the session. The command refuses a score that is
not a whole number from 1 to 4, and a domain with no score. That is why every domain needs a
whole number before the show of hands: a gap cannot be filled in afterwards, and you would take
session 5 again with a later group.

### Who sees it

The comparison is formative. It is not an observation, it is not a calibration, it never counts
toward your clearance, and only the technical training function sees it: your manager does not,
and neither does anyone running a performance review.

### What you keep for later

- **Box R.1.** The anchor you will listen for the next time you are observed.
- **Boxes 2.1 and 4.1.** If the same domain came back on both recordings, that is the domain to
  read again before you are observed, and before you observe anyone yourself.
- **The sessions around this one.** If you take session 5 before the others, listen in their
  feedback rounds for the anchor words you found today. If you take it last, you have now seen
  the anchors you were scored on from the observer's side.

### How this is marked

It is not marked, and there is no pass mark. Session 5 counts as complete once the coordinator has
recorded your comparison. The course is complete when all five sessions are recorded.
