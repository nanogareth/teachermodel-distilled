# Train-the-trainer session 1: Micro-teach rounds -- Run Sheet

> The only document you need on the day. Participant-facing content lives in `LEARNER.md`; the step
> numbers below refer to its step list. Course design: `docs/train-the-trainer/DESIGN.md`, row 1.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Envelope** | 90 min, one continuous block. Two rounds on one clock, no break. |
| **Mode** | In person. One trainer and one group of 4 to 6, split into pods A and B at opposite ends of one room |
| **Roles** | Per slot: one teacher, one or two peers who act as learners and score, and you in one of the two pods. Peer 2 keeps time |
| **Tools** | No software. The printed form from `teachermodel paper` (decision 0022), a timer and a whiteboard or flipchart per pod |
| **Slices** | Each participant's own ten minutes from a technical session they will teach later, taken from its unit |
| **Evidence** | Both rounds' forms, scanned into the private store by the coordinator. Formative only: not observations, never counted toward clearance |
| **Recording** | None. Nothing is filmed, and the forms are the only record |
| **Hardware / cost** | One large room (or two adjacent rooms), two timers, about 36 printed forms for a group of six |

**Both pods run on one clock.** They teach in the same slots, so one call from you moves both pods to
the next turn. You watch one pod per slot, following the rotation tables in Phase 1 and Phase 3, and
you score each participant in one of their two rounds. Peers score every teach.

**Every participant teaches twice.** Round 1 is the full ten-minute slice and five minutes of
feedback. Round 2 is a five-minute reteach of the part the feedback was about, with one planned
change, and three minutes of feedback on whether the change showed.

---

## BEFORE

### What you need in your head

- **The forms exist for the reteach.** A score says which anchor best describes ten minutes of
  teaching. It says nothing about the person, and it never reaches a clearance file: the forms are
  formative, they are not observations, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005).
  Expect someone to ask whether the scores count. The answer is no, and you say it at 0:01.
- **Why two pods.** Six people teaching ten minutes and then reteaching five is ninety minutes of
  teaching before anyone gives feedback, and you can watch one teach at a time. So the pods run side
  by side, peers score every teach, and you score each participant once. Your rotation gives pod A
  your model of the routine in slot 1 and pod B in slot 2.
- **Score what the slice showed.** A domain whose moment never came up is "not seen": the score box
  stays blank and the evidence note says "not seen". A moment that came up and was missed gets a
  score, usually 1. Remote and inclusive delivery is not seen in this session, because nobody is
  remote; session 4 scores it. Lab facilitation is not seen unless the slice has hands-on work.
- **Expect 1s and 2s, and tell the room so.** The anchors describe a whole delivered session. A
  slice that is an explanation followed by "any questions?" scores 1 on prior knowledge and 1 on
  conceptual questioning. Either makes a good focus domain, because a fix for it fits in a
  five-minute reteach.
- **You may not know the subject.** Score technical depth on what anyone in the room can see: a
  question that got an answer, or "I would test it by...", or a question that was deflected.
  Accuracy on the content is checked later, at the dry run (W3) and in the observations that lead to
  clearance. If you do spot an error the teacher left standing, name it in your turn before anything
  else.
- **The routine is the thing they keep.** Run it the same way every time: silent scores first, then
  the teacher, then the peers, then you, then the teacher again. Half the slots run without you, and
  sessions 4 and 5 use the same routine.

- [ ] **Stack the forms** by pod and slot: for each slot, that teacher's round-1 forms on top and
      round-2 forms underneath.
- [ ] **Book the room.** One room large enough for two groups to talk at once, with a whiteboard or
      flipchart at each end. If you cannot get one, book two adjacent rooms and give each pod's

- [ ] Print the two rotation tables (Phase 1 and Phase 3 below) for yourself.

| Group | Pod A | Pod B | Slot 3 | Your pod in round 1, then round 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 3 | 3 | Both pods teach | A, B, A, then B, A, B |
| 5 | 3 | 2 | Pod B has nobody left to teach, so its two join pod A as learners. They do not score | A, B, A, then B, A, A |
| 4 | 2 | 2 | Nobody left to teach in either pod: see the note below | A, B, then B, A |

Each of these rotations puts you in front of every participant at least once. **With four,** each

slot 3 (0:36-0:51) to start the replan early and spend three minutes with each participant. Use
round 2's slot 3 (1:14-1:22) to start the share-out early and give each participant a full minute.

## DURING

### Demo list

Declared per the cadence rule. A phase gets a 5-minute demo when it introduces a software tool no
earlier phase used (**4a**) or needs a non-obvious feature interaction (**4b**). This session uses
no software, so every phase gets a 60-second gate-check.

| Phase | Tool | Demo | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teaching -- no tool | gate-check | n/a; no software. You model the feedback routine live in slot 1 (pod A) and slot 2 (pod B) |
| 2 | Paper plan -- no tool | gate-check | n/a; individual writing on the plan in `LEARNER.md` |
| 3 | Teaching -- no tool | gate-check | n/a; the same routine as Phase 1, shortened |

### The clock

Three gate-checks at 1 minute, 74 minutes of teaching, feedback and replanning in three phases, and
13 minutes of welcome, share-out, reflection and wrap. That is 90 exactly, and there is no slack. If
you are running late, take the minutes from the share-out and then from the reflection. Never shorten
a slot: a teach that loses two minutes is a different teach from the one the participant planned.

| From | To | Min | Segment | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 0:05 | 5 | Welcome, pods, the form and the routine | framing |
| 0:05 | 0:06 | 1 | GATE-CHECK -- Phase 1, round 1 routine | gate-check |
| 0:06 | 0:51 | 45 | Phase 1 work -- round 1, teach and feedback in three slots | work |
| 0:51 | 0:52 | 1 | GATE-CHECK -- Phase 2, the replan | gate-check |
| 0:52 | 0:57 | 5 | Phase 2 work -- replan the reteach | work |
| 0:57 | 0:58 | 1 | GATE-CHECK -- Phase 3, round 2 routine | gate-check |
| 0:58 | 1:22 | 24 | Phase 3 work -- round 2, reteach and feedback in three slots | work |
| 1:22 | 1:26 | 4 | Share-out: what moved | share-out |
| 1:26 | 1:28 | 2 | Reflection | reflection |
| 1:28 | 1:30 | 2 | Wrap and form collection | wrap |

**The phases are the participants' work.** In Phases 1 and 3 they teach, learn and score; in Phase 2
they plan. You score one pod at a time and call time for both.

### 0:00-0:05 Welcome, pods, the form and the routine (plenary)

- **0:00** Everyone in the middle of the room, standing. Point out the two ends: pod A, pod B.
- **0:01** Frame the session: *"Everyone teaches twice today. First your ten minutes, then five
  minutes of feedback on the rubric. Later you reteach five of those minutes with one change, and we
  see whether the change showed. The scores are practice. They are not observations, they never count
  toward clearance, and only the training function sees them."*
- **0:02** Hold up a form: *"Five domains, four anchors each. Score only what the ten minutes showed.
  If a domain had no moment in the slice, leave it blank and write 'not seen'. Remote delivery is not
  seen today. Expect 1s and 2s: the anchors describe a whole session, and you are teaching ten
  minutes of one."*
- **0:03** Walk through the round 1 routine on the pod card: one minute of silent scoring, then
  teacher, peer 1, peer 2, trainer, teacher. Say the stem once: *"I scored [domain] at [number]. The
  anchor says [its words]. What I saw was [what happened, and roughly when]."*
- **0:04** Read out the pods and positions, and say where you start: pod A for slot 1, pod B for
  slot 2.

> Watch for a participant who arrives without a slice. They teach ten minutes of something they
> could explain at a whiteboard tomorrow, and the form says so in its evidence notes. Do not
> reorder the slots to give them time.

#### GATE-CHECK -- Phase 1, round 1 routine (1 min, broadcast)

Everyone moves to their pod. Say this to the whole room as they go.

*"Round 1 is three slots of fifteen minutes, and each slot runs steps 1 to 3. Ten minutes of
teaching, and the timekeeper stops you at ten even if you are mid-sentence. Peers, you are learners
first: do what the teacher asks, and ask one real question each. Then one minute of silent scoring,
then the feedback round in the order on your card. Teachers, take the forms when your feedback ends.
Position 1, set up now."*

### 0:06-0:51 Phase 1 -- round 1, teach and feedback (two pods, three slots)

| Slot | Teaching | Silent scoring | Feedback round | You are in pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:06-0:16 | 0:16-0:17 | 0:17-0:21 | A |
| 2 | 0:21-0:31 | 0:31-0:32 | 0:32-0:36 | B |
| 3 | 0:36-0:46 | 0:46-0:47 | 0:47-0:51 | A |

In each slot, **peer 1** is the person who teaches next (in slot 3, position 1) and **peer 2** is the
remaining person, who keeps time. Peer 1 speaks first in the feedback round so that they can set up
for their own teach while the rest of the round runs.

**The round 1 feedback round (5 minutes):**

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score | Everyone except the teacher | 1:00 | Silent. A score or "not seen" for each domain, and one line of evidence for each score. Nobody talks |
| 1 | Teacher | 0:30 | What they meant to happen, and the moment it went differently |
| 2 | Peer 1 | 0:30 | One domain from prior knowledge or conceptual questioning, in the stem |
| 3 | Peer 2 | 0:30 | One domain from technical depth or lab facilitation, in the stem; if neither was seen, any domain peer 1 did not take |
| 4 | You | 1:30 | Your turn, below. In a pod without you, the teacher picks the focus domain and each peer offers one change aimed at the next anchor up (45 s each) |
| 5 | Teacher | 1:00 | Names the focus domain and the one change for the reteach, writes both in box 1.1, and takes the forms |

**Your turn, in this order:**

1. If you saw a content error that the teacher left standing, name it and the correction. Fifteen
   seconds, no more.
2. Give your own score on each domain the peers spoke to. If yours differs from a peer's by two or
   more, read the anchor wording that decided it for you. Do not argue the score; the anchors decide
   it.
3. Name one focus domain: one where the slice had a moment for it, the score is 1 or 2, and a change
   fits in five minutes. Read the anchor at your score and the anchor you want the reteach to aim
   at, usually one point up. Say what that anchor would look like in this slice, at a named minute.

*"Prior knowledge, I scored 1. The anchor says 'no check of what the room knows; one pace for
everyone.' You started explaining at minute 1 without asking anything. Aim at 3: 'runs the
pre-check, names what it showed, and skips or adds worked examples accordingly.' In this slice that
would be one question at minute 1 about what they already know about retries, a sentence on what the
answers showed, and skipping the worked example if most of them got it right."*

**While the teach runs:**

- Stay outside the learners' line of sight, with the plan and your form. Write minute stamps as
  things happen: "min 4: asks 'make sense?', moves on with no answer".
- Do not ask questions and do not join in. The peers are the learners. A question from you changes
  the teach you are scoring.
- Score alone. The form's own footer asks observers not to compare before both records are made,
  and the silent minute is where that happens.

**Where to look in ten minutes.** The anchors are on the form; this table says where in a slice the
evidence for them shows up.

| Domain | Look at |
|---|---|
| Technical depth | Each learner question. Was it answered, answered with "I would test it by...", or deflected? Did an unsure answer get followed up? |
| Prior knowledge | The first two minutes. Did the teacher find out what the room knew before explaining, and did anything change because of the answer? |
| Conceptual questioning | Every question the teacher asked. Recall, rhetorical, or one with a wrong answer worth hearing? Did the teacher wait, or answer it themselves? |
| Lab facilitation | Only if learners did hands-on work. What happened when something failed? |
| Remote and inclusive | Not seen in this session. Leave it blank and write "not seen; session 4" |

| Broadcast at | Say | Step gate |
|---|---|---|
| ~0:16 | "Ten minutes. Stop teaching now, even mid-sentence. One minute of silent scoring, and nobody talks. That is step 2." | step 1 done |
| ~0:17 | "Pens down. Feedback round in the order on your card, teacher first. That is step 3." | step 2 done |
| ~0:21 | "Slot 2. Forms to the slot 1 teacher. Position 2, start teaching. That is step 1 again." | step 3 done |
| ~0:31 | "Ten minutes. Stop now. Silent scoring, step 2." | step 1 done |
| ~0:36 | "Slot 3. Forms to the slot 2 teacher. Position 3, start teaching. Step 1." | step 3 done |
| ~0:46 | "Ten minutes. Stop now. Silent scoring, step 2." | step 1 done |

Call each one loudly enough for both ends of the room. Between calls, walk to the other pod only on
the rotation, during the last thirty seconds of a feedback round.

> **A peer who says "it was really clear" has not given feedback yet.** Ask, there and then:
> *"Which domain, what score, and which words on the form?"* Do the same in the other pod's first
> slot if you hear it from across the room. The stem is printed on the pod card, so point at it.

> **If a teacher argues with a score,** let them finish their sentence and then move to the next
> turn. The score stays on the form. They can ask one question in turn 5.

#### GATE-CHECK -- Phase 2, the replan (1 min, broadcast)

*"Round 1 is over. For the next five minutes everyone works alone, on steps 4 and 5. Read your
forms, pick the focus domain, and plan five minutes of your slice to reteach with the change in it.
The last line of your plan is what a peer should see if the change worked. I will come round to
some of you."*

### 0:52-0:57 Phase 2 -- replan the reteach (individual, in your pod)

Participants stay at their end of the room and write the reteach plan in `LEARNER.md` (box 2.1).

**Visit the participants you did not score in round 1.** With six, that is pod A position 2 and pod
B positions 1 and 3. Ninety seconds each, three questions:

1. "What did your peers score on the domain you picked, and what did the anchor say?"
2. "What exactly will you do differently, and at what minute of the five?"
3. "What will a peer see if it worked?"

If the change is a feeling ("be more interactive"), ask for the sentence they will say or the thing
they will ask the room to do. If the change is three changes, ask them to keep one.

| Broadcast at | Say | Step gate |
|---|---|---|
| ~0:53 | "One minute in. You should have your focus domain picked. Now plan the five minutes. That is step 5." | step 4 done |
| ~0:56 | "One minute left. Write the last line: what a peer will see if the change worked." | step 5 closing |

#### GATE-CHECK -- Phase 3, round 2 routine (1 min, broadcast)

*"Round 2 is three slots of eight minutes: steps 6 to 8. Five minutes of reteaching, the same
positions in the same order, and the timekeeper stops you at five. Then one minute of silent scoring
on a new form, then two minutes of feedback. In the feedback round, peers go first, and the first
thing they say is whether the change showed. Position 1, set up now."*

### 0:58-1:22 Phase 3 -- round 2, reteach and feedback (two pods, three slots)

| Slot | Reteaching | Silent scoring | Feedback round | You are in pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:58-1:03 | 1:03-1:04 | 1:04-1:06 | B |
| 2 | 1:06-1:11 | 1:11-1:12 | 1:12-1:14 | A |
| 3 | 1:14-1:19 | 1:19-1:20 | 1:20-1:22 | B |

**The round 2 feedback round (3 minutes):**

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score | Everyone except the teacher | 1:00 | Silent, on a new form. Focus domain first, then any other domain the five minutes showed |
| 1 | Peer 1 | 0:30 | "Your focus was [domain]. In round 1 I scored it [number]; now [number]. The anchor for [number] says [its words]. What I saw was [what happened]." |
| 2 | Peer 2 | 0:30 | The same stem, on the focus domain or on another domain that changed |
| 3 | You | 0:45 | Whether the change showed, by the anchor; then one thing to keep for the real session. In a pod without you, the peers take 20 s each and the last 5 s go to the teacher |
| 4 | Teacher | 0:15 | One sentence: what they will keep |

**Your round 2 turn.** If you did not see this participant's round 1, say so, and score the reteach
on its own. Use their box 1.1 and the peers' first turn for what changed. If the change did not
show, say what you saw instead and which anchor it matched. Do not tell them it nearly worked.

> **A reteach that is the whole slice, faster,** has no change to score. Score it as it is and say in
> your turn which five minutes the plan named.

| Broadcast at | Say | Step gate |
|---|---|---|
| ~1:03 | "Five minutes. Stop reteaching now. Silent scoring on a new form, step 7." | step 6 done |
| ~1:04 | "Feedback round: peers first, and start with whether the change showed. Step 8." | step 7 done |
| ~1:06 | "Slot 2. Forms to the slot 1 teacher. Position 2, reteach. Step 6." | step 8 done |
| ~1:11 | "Five minutes. Stop now. Step 7." | step 6 done |
| ~1:14 | "Slot 3. Forms to the slot 2 teacher. Position 3, reteach. Step 6." | step 8 done |
| ~1:19 | "Five minutes. Stop now. Step 7." | step 6 done |

### 1:22-1:26 Share-out: what moved (plenary)

- **1:22** Both pods to the middle of the room. Pod A in position order, then pod B. Thirty seconds
  each: the focus domain, its round 1 and round 2 scores from the peers, and what moved it or did
  not.
- **1:25** Ask for a show of hands on each focus domain. Then say which sessions work on the domains
  with the most hands: session 2 on prior knowledge and conceptual questioning, session 3 on lab
  facilitation, session 4 on remote delivery, and session 5 on scoring itself.

> A participant whose score did not move has still done the session. Say plainly that one five-minute
> reteach is a single attempt, and that the change goes into their plan for session 4.

### 1:26-1:28 Reflection (silent)

Participants write boxes R.1 and R.2 in `LEARNER.md` and read their round 2 forms. You start counting
forms at each end of the room while they write.

1. Which change will you keep for the real session, and at what point in that session does it go?
2. Which anchor did you find hardest to score a peer on, and why?

### 1:28-1:30 Wrap

- **1:28** Collect every form, both rounds, from every teacher. Count them against the pod stacks
  before anyone leaves: up to six per participant.
- **1:29** Say where they go: *"These go to the coordinator today and are kept in the private store
  as your session 1 record. They are not observations, they do not count toward clearance, and only
  the training function sees them. Keep your slice plan and your reteach plan: session 2 writes a
  diagnostic question for this slice, and session 4 asks you to teach it remotely."*
- **1:30** End.

### Recovery moves

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| **A teacher passes ten minutes** | The timekeeper stops them at ten, mid-sentence if need be. The overrun is evidence on the form. Never extend a slot; the next slot's start is fixed. |
| **You are 3+ minutes late at 0:51** | Take the time from the share-out (down to two minutes), then from the reflection. Do not shorten the replan or any slot. |
| **The pods are too loud for each other** | Move one pod to a corridor or an adjacent room. Its timekeeper runs a timer from your last call, and you walk across at each rotation change. |
| **A laptop slice will not connect** | They teach it at the whiteboard. The clock does not stop, and the evidence note says the plan changed. |
| **A participant is absent on the day** | Rebuild the pods from the group-size table before 0:04. Do not run a pod of one. |
| **Peers give praise instead of a score** | *"Which domain, what score, and which words on the form?"* Then wait. |
| **A peer and you differ by two or more** | Read the anchor wording that decided yours, once, and move on. Note the domain for the capture list. |
| **A participant takes feedback badly** | Hold the routine: the score is about ten minutes of teaching. Offer to talk after the session. Do not change a score to ease the moment. |
| **Someone asks whether the scores count** | No. They are formative, they are not observations, they never count toward clearance, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005). |
| **You are pulled out of a pod** | That pod runs turn 4 without you, as the card says. Score the participant in the other round instead. |

## AFTER

### Close protocol

- [ ] Count the forms again away from the room: every participant should have one to three per
      round, each with an Instructor id, an Observer id and the round written in.
- [ ] Hand them to the coordinator the same day in a closed folder. The forms hold scores against
      instructor ids: do not photograph them and do not leave them in the room.
- [ ] The coordinator scans each participant's forms into one PDF (or PNG or JPEG) and takes it in
      with the date of the session (decision 0030):
      `teachermodel course intake <scan> --instructor tm:instructor/<id> --session micro-teach --on <YYYY-MM-DD>`
      It stores the scan under a key of its own and records the session; an empty or wrong file is
      returned with its problems printed, and shows as returned on the coordinator board.
- [ ] Keep the paper until the head of training says what happens to it. The retention schedule
      (`standards/retention.yaml`) has no entry for training forms yet.

### If a participant missed the session

They join the next group's session 1. There is no solo route: the session needs peers to teach to
and peers to score, and the course record takes a session only once it has happened.

### What participants take forward

- **Session 2** writes a diagnostic question, from the unit's misconceptions list, for the same
  slice.
- **Session 4** has them teach this slice again remotely, with the same routine.
- **Session 5** scores reference recordings on the same anchors. Participants who found the anchors
  hard to choose between today will recognise the problem there.

### Capture for next run

Counts only, never names.

- [ ] Whether the slots ran to time, and which turn overran
- [ ] How often a peer's score and yours differed by two or more, and on which domain. This is early
      calibration data for session 5
- [ ] Which domains were picked as focus, and how often the round 2 score on the focus domain moved
- [ ] Whether pod B's first slot, which runs without your model, kept to the routine
- [ ] Whether "not seen" was used for domains with no moment in the slice, and nowhere else
- [ ] Update the session log
