### What this session is for

You know your subject. This session is practice at teaching it, with feedback in the words your
observers will use when you are observed for real. The evidence for microteaching is weak to
moderate, so the course uses it for what it does reliably: short practice, specific feedback, and a
second attempt straight away.

Every later session builds on today. Session 2 writes a diagnostic question for the slice you teach
today, and session 4 has you teach the same slice remotely.

### What you will be able to do

1. Teach a ten-minute slice of a technical session to peers, and stop on time.
2. Score a peer's teaching on the form, choosing between anchors by what you saw.
3. Give feedback as a score, the anchor's own words and the evidence, and take it the same way.
4. Reteach five minutes with one planned change aimed at a named anchor.

### Choosing your slice

- Take ten minutes from a technical session you will teach later. The session's unit lists its
  learning outcomes, and your ten minutes should serve one of them.
- Choose ten minutes in which your learners do or decide something. A slice that is ten minutes of
  you explaining gives your peers almost nothing to score except that.
- Leave out anything that needs a lab environment or a live system. Session 3 covers labs.
- Setup counts toward your ten minutes. If your slice needs a laptop, tell the coordinator, and you
  will be given position 2 or 3 so that you can set up during the slot before yours.
- Your peers are engineers from other specialisms, acting as learners who are new to your topic.
  Choose ten minutes they could follow.

## How the session runs

One continuous 90-minute block in one room. There is no software and no demo: before each round the
trainer gives a one-minute briefing to the whole room, and in the first round you see the trainer
run the feedback routine in your pod.

| When | What runs |
|------|-----------|
| 0:00-0:05 | Welcome, pods, the form and the feedback routine |
| 0:05-0:06 | Briefing: round 1 |
| 0:06-0:51 | **Round 1**: three slots of 15 minutes, ten of teaching and five of feedback |
| 0:51-0:52 | Briefing: the replan |
| 0:52-0:57 | **Replan**: five minutes alone, planning your reteach |
| 0:57-0:58 | Briefing: round 2 |
| 0:58-1:22 | **Round 2**: three slots of 8 minutes, five of reteaching and three of feedback |
| 1:22-1:26 | Share-out, both pods together |
| 1:26-1:28 | Reflection, written |
| 1:28-1:30 | Wrap: the trainer collects every form |

### Pods, positions and slots

The trainer puts you in pod A or pod B, with people from other specialisms, and gives you a
position: 1, 2 or 3. You teach in the slot that matches your position, in both rounds. In every slot
the other members of your pod are **peers**: they act as your learners while you teach and then
score you.

| Slot | Teaches | Peer 1: speaks first, then sets up | Peer 2: keeps time, speaks second |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 |
| 2 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 1 |
| 3 | Position 3 | Position 1 | Position 2 |

In a pod of two, your one peer takes both peer turns.

The trainer is in one pod per slot: pod A in slots 1 and 3 of round 1, pod B in slot 2; then pod B
in slots 1 and 3 of round 2, and pod A in slot 2. So the trainer scores you in one of your two
rounds, and your peers score you in both. If your group is four or five, the trainer tells you how
slot 3 runs.

**As a peer, you are a learner first.** Do what the teacher asks you to do, answer their questions
as yourself, and ask one real question each. Do not play a difficult learner. Jot a minute and a few
words on the form when something happens, and do the rest of your scoring in the silent minute.

### The form

The form is the rubric printed on paper, with the five domains and four anchors for each. Its
header is filled in for you, except **Observer id**: you write your own instructor id there.

- **Score each domain, or write "not seen".** Circle the score whose anchor best matches what
  happened. If the ten minutes had no moment for a domain, leave the score blank and write "not
  seen" in the evidence note. If the moment came up and was missed, that is a score, usually 1.
- **Remote and inclusive delivery is not seen today**, because nobody is remote. Session 4 scores
  it.
- **Lab facilitation is not seen** unless the slice had hands-on work.
- **Write one line of evidence for every score**: what happened and roughly when. For example,
  "min 6: asked why the loop needs a step limit, answered it herself after 2 s".
- **Score alone and in silence.** Do not look at another form before the feedback round. The form's
  footer asks for this, and the silent minute is where it happens.
- **Expect 1s and 2s.** The anchors describe a whole delivered session, and you are seeing ten
  minutes of one. A 1 tells the teacher which anchor to aim at next. It is not a mark.

### The feedback routine, round 1 (5 minutes)

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score | Everyone except the teacher | 1:00 | Silent. A score or "not seen" for each domain, and a line of evidence for each score |
| 1 | Teacher | 0:30 | What you meant to happen, and the moment it went differently |
| 2 | Peer 1 | 0:30 | One domain from prior knowledge or conceptual questioning, in the stem below |
| 3 | Peer 2 | 0:30 | One domain from technical depth or lab facilitation, in the stem; if neither was seen, any domain peer 1 did not take |
| 4 | Trainer | 1:30 | Names one focus domain, reads the anchor at the score and the anchor to aim at, and says what that would look like in your slice. If the trainer is in the other pod: you pick the focus domain, and each peer offers one change aimed at the next anchor up, 45 seconds each |
| 5 | Teacher | 1:00 | You name the focus domain and your one change, write both in box 1.1, and take the forms |

### The feedback routine, round 2 (3 minutes)

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score | Everyone except the teacher | 1:00 | Silent, on a new form. The focus domain first, then any other domain the five minutes showed |
| 1 | Peer 1 | 0:30 | Whether the change showed, in the stem, with the round 1 and round 2 scores on the focus domain |
| 2 | Peer 2 | 0:30 | The same, or another domain that changed |
| 3 | Trainer | 0:45 | Whether the change showed by the anchor, and one thing to keep for the real session. Without the trainer: 20 seconds per peer, and the last 5 seconds pass to the teacher |
| 4 | Teacher | 0:15 | One sentence: what you will keep |

### How to say a score

Use the same stem every time, in your own voice:

> "I scored [domain] at [number]. The anchor for [number] says [its words]. What I saw was [what
> happened, and roughly when]."

Then, if you have time, the anchor to aim at and what it would look like in this slice. An invented
example, from a slice on stop conditions in a tool-using agent loop:

> "I scored conceptual questioning at 2. The anchor for 2 says 'asks conceptual questions but
> answers them without discussion'. At about minute 6 you asked why the loop needs a step limit,
> waited two seconds and answered it yourself. The 3 anchor wants a question whose wrong answers map
> to known misconceptions, and a change of course on the result. Here that could be a show of hands
> on two answers, one of them 'the model retries a failed tool call by itself', before you explain."

"It was really clear" is not feedback yet, because it names no domain or anchor. When you are the
teacher, listen without defending. You can ask one question in your closing turn, such as "at which
minute?".

## After the session

### What happens to your forms

The trainer collects every form at 1:28, from both rounds, and gives them to the coordinator the
same day. The coordinator keeps them in the private store as your session 1 record and marks the
session as recorded on your course record.

The forms are formative. They are not observations, they never count toward your clearance, and
only the technical training function sees them: your manager does not, and neither does anyone
running a performance review. Your clearance comes later, from observations of real deliveries.

### What you keep for later sessions

- **Your slice plan and box 2.1.** Session 2 writes a diagnostic question for this slice, using the
  unit's list of misconceptions. Session 4 has you teach the slice again, remotely.
- **Box R.2.** Session 5 asks you to score recordings on the same anchors, and the anchor you found
  hardest today is a good one to watch for there.

### How this is marked

It is not marked, and there is no pass mark. Session 1 counts as complete once the coordinator has
recorded it. The course is complete when all five sessions are recorded, and then you move on to
shadowing a cleared instructor.
