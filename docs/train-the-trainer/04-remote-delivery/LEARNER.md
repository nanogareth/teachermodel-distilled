### What this session is for

When some learners are in the room and some are on a call, the room gets the lesson first. The
person beside you answers before the call has heard the question. A show of hands leaves out
everyone who is not in the room. A diagram on the shared screen is too small to read on a laptop,
and a question asked from the back of the room never reaches the microphone. Nobody planned any of
it; it is what happens when the instructor teaches the room they can see.

The rubric describes four ways it can go. The remote learners are never addressed. They are asked
at the end of each activity, once the room has answered. They get the same questions, the same
checks and the same help queue as the room. Or the instructor plans the pacing and the checks for
each mode, and changes them when the participation data say so.

Today you practise the third and fourth, in a slot short enough that every minute shows.

### What you will be able to do

1. Run a vote, a pair task and a check so that a remote learner takes part in each as fully as a
   learner in the room.
2. Read where the room is from data (poll counts, the chat, raised hands, the room page) rather
   than from faces, and change your plan because of it.
3. Make a session usable for everyone: captions, visuals described aloud, nothing that depends on
   colour alone, the materials as a file.
4. Score a peer's teach on the remote and inclusive anchors, from the room and from the remote
   spot.

### Hybrid, inside one building

The room page from session 3 is served from the trainer's laptop, and only laptops on the same
network can reach it. So today's remote learners are in the building: at a quiet desk or in a small
room a minute's walk from the pod room, on their own laptop and headset, where they cannot hear the
room. Everything they get comes through the call.

There are three calls on the organisation's video tool, and the invitations are in your calendar:
"TTT-4 whole group" for Phase 1, and one for each pod. Each has polls, chat, a raised-hand button,
breakout rooms and live captions. Nothing is recorded, and the trainer turns off any saved
transcript of the captions.

**How you join depends on where you are.**

- **In a room:** join on your laptop with your microphone and speaker **off**. You vote in the
  poll, ask for help through the call, and read the chat. The facilitator's laptop is the room's
  microphone and speaker, because two open microphones in one room make an echo.
- **At a remote spot:** join with your headset, muted until you speak, captions on.
- **In a breakout room, from the room:** headset on, move away from the others, then unmute.

### What parity means in practice

| | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Same question** | One poll for every vote, and the room votes in it too. No show of hands |
| **Who answers first** | Alternates: the call first on one question, the room first on the next |
| **Same check** | Whatever you use to see who has finished or who is stuck, the call is in it |
| **Same help queue** | The raised hand on the call, or "help" in the chat, for everyone. Nobody has to unmute to ask, and in-room learners use it too |
| **Captions** | On from the first minute, on the room's screen and on the call |
| **Visuals** | Everything on the screen is also said aloud. Nothing depends on colour alone: say "four chose B", not "most went green" |
| **Materials** | Posted in the chat as a file before you start, so the remote learner can open and zoom it |
| **Questions from the room** | Repeated into the microphone before you answer them |
| **Pacing** | The call is a few seconds behind the room. Wait five seconds after a question before you take an answer, and leave a poll open until everyone has voted |

### How you are scored

Remote and inclusive delivery has four anchors on the form:

| Score | Anchor | What it looks like in a six-minute teach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remote learners are not addressed | The person on the call is never asked anything, and finds out about the pair task from what the microphone picks up |
| 2 | Remote learners are addressed at the end of activities only | "Anything from the call?", after the room has answered. Or the room votes by hand and the call in the poll |
| 3 | Remote learners get the same questions, checks and help queue as the room | One poll for both, a question to the call in its turn, help taken in the order of one queue |
| 4 | Pacing and checks are planned per mode and adjusted from participation data | As 3, and the plan allows for the call's delay, and the facilitator changes something because the counts or the chat said so |

The inclusive part is scored in the same domain. A visual that is never described, a handout only
on the shared screen, or captions left off is evidence against a 3.

The other four domains are scored only when your six minutes had a moment for them. Your poll is
usually a moment for conceptual questioning, and what you do with its counts is one for prior
knowledge. Everything else is "not seen".

### Your six minutes

Take six minutes of your session 1 slice that include your session 2 question. Replan them for a
hybrid room. A plan that works usually looks like this:

| Minute | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-1 | Captions on, the file in the chat, the remote learner greeted by name and asked a question they can answer in the chat |
| 1-3 | Your question as a poll for both learners. Read the counts aloud, then ask one learner why someone would choose a wrong option: the call first, or the room first and the call next |
| 3-5 | A pair task: the in-room learner and the remote learner together in a breakout room. Say the task and post it in the chat before you open the room |
| 5-6 | One check you read from data, such as a second poll, a line each in the chat, or the room page if your slice has a lab with checkpoints. Say what it showed and what you do next because of it |

Breakout rooms take about fifteen seconds to open and the same to close. Plan for it.

## How the session runs

One continuous 90-minute block. The trainer teaches a hybrid segment first, with the whole group
as learners and two of you on the call, so that you see each move before you make it. Before each
phase the trainer gives a one-minute briefing.

| When | What runs |
|------|-----------|
| 0:00-0:05 | Welcome: parity and the four anchors |
| 0:05-0:06 | Briefing: the trainer's segment |
| 0:06-0:17 | **Phase 1**: the trainer teaches a hybrid segment of the lab, then you take it apart |
| 0:17-0:18 | Briefing: planning |
| 0:18-0:26 | **Phase 2**: finish your six-minute plan and set up your pod's call |
| 0:26-0:27 | Briefing: hybrid rounds |
| 0:27-1:16 | **Phase 3**: three slots per pod, the two pods taking turns |
| 1:16-1:17 | Briefing: your real session |

**Position 1s start remote.** If you are position 1 in your pod, go straight to your pod's remote
spot when you arrive and join the whole-group call from there. You come back to room A at 0:17.

### Pods, positions and slots

You are in the same pod and position as in session 1, and you teach in the slot that matches your
position. Pod A teaches in room A and pod B in room B. Each pod has its own remote spot.

| Slot | Facilitator, in the room | In-room learner, keeps time | Remote learner, at the remote spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 |
| 2 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 1 |
| 3 | Position 3 | Position 1 | Position 2 |

**At each changeover two people change places.** The remote learner walks into the room with their
form. The facilitator who has just taught takes their laptop and headset to the remote spot and
joins the call from there. After slot 3 the remote learner walks back.

If your group is four or five, the trainer tells you how the slots work.

### The two pods take turns

The pods run seven minutes apart, so that the trainer can watch every teach. Your pod card has the
real clock times.

| Time | Pod A | Pod B |
|---|---|---|
| 0:27-0:34 | Slot 1: teach, then silent scoring | Set up: test the call; position 3 goes to the remote spot |
| 0:34-0:41 | Slot 1: feedback, then changeover | Slot 1: teach, then silent scoring |
| 0:41-0:48 | Slot 2: teach, then silent scoring | Slot 1: feedback, then changeover |
| 0:48-0:55 | Slot 2: feedback, then changeover | Slot 2: teach, then silent scoring |
| 0:55-1:02 | Slot 3: teach, then silent scoring | Slot 2: feedback, then changeover |
| 1:02-1:09 | Slot 3: feedback; the remote learner walks back | Slot 3: teach, then silent scoring |
| 1:09-1:16 | The trainer's score, one at a time; read your forms; start box 4.1 | Slot 3: feedback; the remote learner walks back |

### The slot

| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Teach | 6:00 | The facilitator teaches their six minutes. The in-room learner stops it at six, finished or not |
| Silent scoring | 1:00 | Both learners score on the form, remote and inclusive delivery first |
| Feedback round | 4:00 | In the order below, the remote learner first |
| Changeover | 3:00 | Two people change places, and the next facilitator sets up |

**As a learner, take part as yourself.** Vote, answer, ask for help when you are stuck, and ask one
real question. In the room, use the call for all of it: the poll for votes, the raised hand or the
chat for help. At the remote spot, stay there until the changeover, even if the call goes quiet.

### The form

The form is the one from sessions 1 to 3. Write your own instructor id as **Observer id**. Score
remote and inclusive delivery first. Then score any other domain the six minutes had a moment for,
and write "not seen" for the rest. Write one line of evidence for every score: what happened, and
roughly at what minute. Score alone and in silence.

**At the remote spot,** your forms are in the folder there, one per slot, marked on the outside.
Fill in the one for this slot during the silent minute, and bring it with you at the changeover. It
goes in the pod folder in the room. Never photograph a form or type a score into the chat: the
forms hold scores against instructor ids, and the call may keep its chat.

### The feedback routine (4 minutes)

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remote learner, over the call | 1:15 | Remote and inclusive delivery, in the stem: one moment you got the same as the room, and one when you got it later or not at all, with the minute |
| 2 | In-room learner | 1:00 | Remote and inclusive delivery, or another domain the six minutes showed, in the stem |
| 3 | Remote learner, then in-room learner | 0:45 | One change each, aimed at the next anchor up |
| 4 | Facilitator | 1:00 | What you planned for the call, and the moment it went differently. You name the one change you will keep and write it in box 3.1. The in-room learner's form goes in the pod folder |

The trainer is not in the feedback round, because by then they are watching the other pod teach.
They score you on their own form and tell you their score one to one at the end: pod A at 1:09,
pod B in Phase 4.

### How to say a score

The stem is the session 1 stem:

> "I scored remote and inclusive delivery at [number]. The anchor for [number] says [its words].
> What I saw was [what happened, and roughly when]."

Two invented examples, from teaches of other slices:

> "I scored remote and inclusive delivery at 2. The anchor for 2 says 'remote learners are
> addressed at the end of activities only'. At minute 2 you asked for hands up, and the person
> beside you answered. Then you said 'anything from the call?', and by then I had nothing to add."

> "I scored remote and inclusive delivery at 3. The anchor for 3 says 'remote learners get the same
> questions, checks and help queue as the room'. At minute 1 we both voted in the poll, at minute 2
> you asked me first, and at minute 4 my 'help' in the chat was answered before the room's question,
> because it came first."

## After the session

### What happens to your forms

The trainer collects every form at 1:28, from the pod folders and the remote spots, and gives them
to the coordinator the same day. This time the forms are the record for the session: the
coordinator scans yours into the private store and records session 4 on your course record, dated
the day of the session.

The forms are formative. They are not observations, they never count toward your clearance, and
only the technical training function sees them: your manager does not, and neither does anyone
running a performance review.

Nothing was recorded on any call. If you notice a recording or a saved transcript of a session 4
call, tell the trainer the same day.

### What you keep for later sessions

- **Box 4.1.** It is the plan for your real session in its own mode.
- **Box 3.2 and box R.1.** What it was like on the call. Session 5 asks you to score recordings on
  all five domains, remote and inclusive delivery among them.
- **Your lab copy.** Delete `lab-ttt4` when you like; nothing in it is needed again.

### How this is marked

It is not marked, and there is no pass mark. Session 4 counts as complete once the coordinator has
recorded your forms. The course is complete when all five sessions are recorded.
