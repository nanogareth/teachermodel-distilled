# Train-the-trainer session 4: Remote and inclusive delivery -- Run Sheet

> The only document you need on the day. Participant-facing content lives in `LEARNER.md`; the step
> numbers below refer to its step list. Course design: `docs/train-the-trainer/DESIGN.md`, row 4.
> Phase 1 uses the lab from session 3, `scenarios/aggregates-only-export` in the proving-ground
> repository.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Envelope** | 90 min, one continuous block. Four phases on one clock, no break |
| **Mode** | Hybrid, inside one building. One trainer and one group of 4 to 6, in the session 1 pods. Each pod teaches from its own room, and in every slot one learner joins the pod's call from a remote spot down the corridor |
| **Roles** | In Phase 1 you teach and the group are your learners, two of them on the call. In Phase 3, per slot: one facilitator in the room, one learner beside them who keeps time, one remote learner, and you, watching every teach in turn |
| **Tools** | The organisation's video tool: three calls (whole group, pod A, pod B), each with polls, chat, raised hands, breakout rooms and live captions. The lab's checkpoint collector (the room page) on your laptop for Phase 1. Printed forms from `teachermodel paper` (decision 0022), pod cards and a form folder at each remote spot |
| **Slice** | Six minutes of each participant's session 1 slice, replanned for hybrid delivery, with their session 2 question run as a poll |
| **Evidence** | The remote round's rubric forms: one from each peer and one from you per participant, scanned by the coordinator into the private store and recorded. Formative only: not observations, never counted toward clearance |
| **Recording** | None. Recording is off on all three calls, and so is any saved transcript. You say so at 0:01 |
| **Hardware / cost** | Two rooms next to each other (room A with a large screen), two remote spots out of earshot, one network for all of them; a laptop and a headset with a microphone per participant; a speakerphone per room if you have them; about 22 printed forms for a group of six |

**The pods take turns, seven minutes apart.** Pod A teaches while pod B gives feedback, and then
the other way round. You watch every teach and score every participant once. Peers score every
slot. Your spoken turn moves out of the pod's feedback round into a one-to-one at the end, because
while one pod gives feedback you are watching the other pod teach.

**Everyone is the remote learner at least once.** In each slot the person who has just taught walks
to the remote spot and joins the pod's call from there, and the person who was remote walks back
into the room. The remote learner speaks first in every feedback round.

## BEFORE

### What you need in your head

- **What parity means.** The anchor for 3 says remote learners get the same questions, checks and
  help queue as the room. In practice: one poll that the room votes in too, a question to the call
  before the room has had its say, one help queue (the call's raised hand or a line in chat) that
  in-room learners use as well, captions on, everything on the screen also said aloud, and the
  materials posted as a file in chat, so that nobody depends on a shared screen they cannot zoom.
- **What a 4 needs.** The anchor for 4 says pacing and checks are planned per mode and adjusted from
  participation data. Six minutes can show it: a wait chosen for the call's delay, a poll left open
  until every learner has voted, and a change of plan because the counts or the chat said so.
- **Why hybrid inside the building.** The room page is served from your laptop and only laptops on
  the same network can reach it. There is no relay for learners outside it yet. So the remote
  learner is on the same network, at a desk or in a small room where they cannot hear the pod
  room. If they can hear the room through the wall, they are not remote, and the score means
  nothing.
- **Read the room from data.** In Phase 1 that is the room page. In Phase 3 most slices have no lab,
  so the data are the poll counts, the chat and the raised hands. Only a slice taken from a
  proving-ground lab with a `checkpoints.json` can use a room page in Phase 3, and its facilitator
  runs the collector on their own laptop.
- **Score what the slice showed.** Remote and inclusive delivery is scored on every teach. The other
  four domains are scored only when the six minutes had a moment for them, and are otherwise "not
  seen". The poll usually gives conceptual questioning a moment, and the route the facilitator
  takes after it usually gives prior knowledge one.
- **Nothing is recorded.** Turn recording off on all three calls, and turn off any setting that
  saves a transcript of the captions. The tool may keep the chat after the call, so the chat holds
  help requests and task text only: no scores, and nothing about a learner.
- **The same scores, the same rules.** The forms are formative, they are not observations, they
  never count toward clearance, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005). The
  feedback routine is the session 1 routine with two changes: the remote learner speaks first, and
  your turn comes later, one to one.

### Partner facilitators: everyone remote

Partner facilitators take this session fully remote, and for them it is the main session rather
than an adaptation (`DESIGN.md`, "Partner facilitators"). Run the same clock with these changes.

- **Everyone joins from their own desk,** on the same three calls. There are no rooms, no remote
  spots and no walking. The three-minute changeover in each slot stays, for the next facilitator to
  set up their poll and post their file.
- **Parity is between the people on the call.** With no room to compare against, anchor 3 asks
  whether every learner gets the same question, the same check and a place in the same help queue.
  Watch who is asked first, whose help request is taken in order, and whether a learner with the
  camera off is ever asked anything.
- **The room page cannot be used** until a relay exists, because the collector only serves the local
  network. In Phase 1, read step 1 two ways instead. Run a poll, "Step 1: A PASS, B not yet, C

- **Who scores.** You score each participant once, moving between the two pod calls on the same

- **Check the lab at T-7.** The remote version of session 3 has no pack yet, so partners may not

## DURING

### Demo list

Declared per the cadence rule. A phase gets a 5-minute demo when it introduces a software tool no
earlier phase used (**4a**) or needs a non-obvious feature interaction (**4b**). Phase 1 introduces
the video tool's poll, breakout rooms and captions alongside the lab and its room page, which is a
4a trigger. It gets a gate-check and no separate demo, because Phase 1 is itself the demonstration:
you teach a hybrid segment with the participants as your learners, in the room and on the call, and
they use every one of those features as you do. A demo card before it would show the same seven
minutes twice.

| 3 | Video call | gate-check | n/a; the tools from Phase 1, used again |
| 4 | Paper -- no tool | gate-check | n/a; individual writing in `LEARNER.md` |

phases, and 13 minutes of welcome, share-out, reflection and wrap. That is 90 exactly. If you are
running late, take the minutes from the share-out and then from the reflection. Never shorten a
Phase 3 half: the other pod's half starts on time whatever happens, and you have to be there.

### 0:00-0:05 Welcome: parity and the four anchors (plenary)

- **0:00** Everyone in room A, except the two position 1s, who are at the remote spots on the
  whole-group call. Say hello to the call first, by spot: *"Remote spot A, can you hear me and see
  the captions? Put a yes in the chat."* Wait for both before you go on.
- **0:01** Frame the session: *"Today each of you teaches six minutes of your session 1 slice to
  two learners. One is beside you in the room. The other is on the call from a remote spot down the
  corridor, and everyone takes a turn there. What we score is whether the person on the call gets
  the same lesson as the person in the room. Nothing today is recorded. Recording is off on all
  three calls, and so is any saved transcript."*
- **0:02** The anchors: *"Remote and inclusive delivery has four anchors. One: the remote learners
  are never addressed. Two: they are asked at the end of an activity, after the room has had its
  say. Three: they get the same questions, the same checks and the same help queue as the room.
  Four: you plan the pacing and the checks for each mode, and you change them when the participation
  data tell you to."*
- **0:03** What parity looks like: *"One poll for every vote, and the room votes in it too. A
  question to the call before the room has answered, and next time the other way round. One help
  queue, the raised hand on the call or a line in chat, for the room as well. Captions on. Anything
  on the screen, you also say. The handout goes in the chat as a file. And you wait a few seconds
  longer than feels natural, because the call is behind the room."*
- **0:04** *"The scores work as they did in session 1. They are practice, they are not
  observations, they never count toward clearance, and only the training function sees them. The
  record for this session is the forms. Your peers score you in the round, and I score everyone
  once and tell you my score one to one at the end."*

> Watch for a remote spot that has not answered by 0:01. Send someone to it at once; the usual cause
> is a laptop that joined with the microphone muted and the captions off, or a remote spot on a
> different network.

### 0:06-0:17 Phase 1 -- you teach a hybrid segment of the lab, then taken apart (plenary)

Seven minutes of teaching, one minute of silent scoring, three minutes taking it apart. The two
position 1s are the remote learners.

- **0:06** The opening, on the call: *"Captions are on. If you are on the call and cannot see them,
  say so in the chat. I have put two things in the chat: the lab's README as a file, and the room
  page address. You can open both yourselves, and I will say out loud everything I put on the
  screen."* Share the room page on the call. *"If you need help at any point, raise your hand on the
  call or type 'help' and your step in the chat. That is the one queue for everyone, and in the
  room too: no waving at me. I take it in order."*
- **0:07** Join and step 1. Post the join command in the chat, write it on the flipchart and read it
  aloud. *"From your lab-ttt4 folder, run this once, then run step 1 with `python checkpoint.py 1`
  and read the last line it prints."* Wait. Read the page aloud, in numbers, not colours, for example:
  *"Room CEDAR: six devices reporting. Step 1 has passed on five, and one device is stuck on it. There are
  six of you."* Then take the queue: *"Whoever is stuck, you are first in the queue: a hand on the
  call or a line in the chat."* Answer them in the order the queue shows, room or call.
- **0:09** The vote. Open the poll. *"One question, and everyone answers in the poll, the room as
  well. I will keep it open until I have six votes."* Read the question and the three options aloud.
  Wait until six votes, or 45 seconds. Read the counts aloud by letter, for example: *"Four chose
  B, two chose A, nobody chose C."* Ask the call first, by spot: *"Remote spot B, why might someone choose A?"*
  Then the room. Then the route call, out loud, from the counts: *"Four of six is enough to go on.
  If most of you had chosen A, I would spend two minutes on what the visible tests check before
  step 2."*
- **0:11** The pair task. Say it, and post it in the chat before you open the rooms, because the
  chat may not reach a breakout room: *"In pairs, one minute: find one line in exporter/export.py
  that could lead back to a learner, and write the name of a test for it with identity in the
  name."* Open breakout rooms of two: each remote spot with one person from the room, and the other
  two people from the room together. *"In the room, headset on, move apart, and unmute in your
  breakout."* At 0:12 send the rooms a 15-second warning, then close them. Opening and closing
  breakout rooms takes about fifteen seconds each way, which is why the task is one minute.
- **0:12:15** Back on the main call. *"Run step 2 now. 'Not yet' is the right answer while the
  exporter still leaks."* Read the page aloud: how many devices have reported step 2. Use it, out
  loud, for example: *"Four devices have reported step 2, and the two remote spots have not. The call is behind
  the room by a few seconds, so I will give you thirty more before I move on."*
- **0:13** *"Stop. One minute: score my segment in box 1.1, remote and inclusive delivery only,
  with one line of evidence."* Stop the collector.
- **0:14** Take it apart, remote spots first: *"Remote spot A, one moment you got the same thing
  as the room, and one moment you got it later or not at all."* Then spot B. Then the room. Then you,
  by the clock:
  - *"At 0:06 the captions, the file and the help queue came before anything else. That is the
    inclusive part, and it costs a minute."*
  - *"At 0:07 I read the page in numbers and counted it against the room. I did not look round at
    faces to find who was stuck: the queue told me."*
  - *"At 0:09 everyone voted in the same poll, I waited for six votes, and the call answered first.
    That is the anchor for 3."*
  - *"At 0:13 I gave thirty more seconds because the remote spots had not reported. A change made
    because of the data, with a reason said out loud, is the anchor for 4."*
- **0:16** Ask for their scores by a show of fingers in the room and in the chat from the remote
  spots. If anyone gave 4, ask what they would have needed to see to give 3, and the other way
  round. Ask the remote spots for theirs first.

### 0:18-0:26 Phase 2 -- finish the six-minute plan and set up the pod call (individual)

Participants work in `LEARNER.md` box 2.1, then on their pod's call.

**While they plan, walk the room.** In each plan, look for these four things in order:

1. **Two channels for one vote.** The room shows hands and the call uses the poll, so the counts
   come at different times and the room's answer is heard first. Ask: *"How does the person beside
   you vote?"*
2. **A remote learner who is first addressed after minute 2.** Ask: *"At what minute does the
   person on the call first hear a question meant for them?"*
3. **A pair task with no plan for sound.** Ask: *"Where does the person in the room talk to the
   person on the call from, and with what?"* The answer is a headset, in a breakout room, away from
   the facilitator's microphone.
4. **A check with no decision in it.** "I'll see how the poll goes" is not a check. Ask: *"What
   number makes you change what you do next, and what do you do then?"*

### 0:27-1:16 Phase 3 -- three slots per pod, staggered: teach, score, feedback, changeover (two pods)

**The roles in each pod (group of six):**

| Slot | Facilitator, in the room | In-room learner, keeps time | Remote learner, at the remote spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 |
| 2 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 1 |
| 3 | Position 3 | Position 1 | Position 2 |

At each changeover the remote learner walks into the room, with their form, and becomes the next
in-room learner. The facilitator who has just taught walks out to the remote spot and joins the call
from there. The in-room learner stays and teaches next. After slot 3 the remote learner walks back.

**The staggered clock.** Each pod runs halves of seven minutes. A teach half is six minutes of
teaching and the silent minute. A feedback half is four minutes of feedback and three of
changeover.

| Half | Time | Pod A (room A) | Pod B (room B) | You |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:27-0:34 | Slot 1: teach 0:27-0:33, silent score 0:33-0:34 | Set up: test poll, captions, position 3 joins from remote spot B | Pod A's teach. At 0:33 go to room B and score there |
| 2 | 0:34-0:41 | Slot 1: feedback 0:34-0:38, changeover 0:38-0:41 | Slot 1: teach 0:34-0:40, silent score 0:40-0:41 | Pod B's teach. At 0:40 go to room A and score there |
| 3 | 0:41-0:48 | Slot 2: teach and silent score | Slot 1: feedback and changeover | Pod A's teach |
| 4 | 0:48-0:55 | Slot 2: feedback and changeover | Slot 2: teach and silent score | Pod B's teach |

**What you do in each teach half:**

| Timer | What you do |
|---|---|
| Before 0:00 | Arrive in the minute before the teach starts. Join the pod's call on your laptop with headphones, microphone off, camera off, so you see and hear what the remote learner gets. Score the teach you have just left, alone, in that minute |
| 0:00-6:00 | Watch from the side of the room, out of the learners' line of sight. Look at the call as often as at the room. Write minute stamps: "min 1: poll opened, both voted", "min 3: asked room first, call not asked" |
| 6:00 | The teach stops. Take your form and go straight to the other pod's room. Do not stay for this pod's silent minute |

**The feedback round (4 minutes),** run by the pod without you:

| Turn | Who | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remote learner, over the call | 1:15 | Remote and inclusive delivery, in the stem: one moment they got the same as the room, and one when they got it later or not at all, with the minute |
| 2 | In-room learner | 1:00 | Remote and inclusive delivery, or another domain the six minutes showed, in the stem |
| 3 | Remote learner, then in-room learner | 0:45 | One change each, aimed at the next anchor up |
| 4 | Facilitator | 1:00 | What they planned for the call and the moment it went differently. Names the one change they will keep and writes it in box 3.1. The in-room learner's form goes in the pod folder |

**The changeover (3 minutes).** The remote learner brings their form into the room and puts it in
the pod folder. The facilitator who has just taught takes their laptop and headset to the remote
spot and joins the call. The next facilitator shares their screen, turns captions on and gets their
poll and file ready. The new in-room learner joins the call with microphone and speaker off.

**Where to look in six minutes.** The anchor wording is on the form; this table says what each
anchor looks like in a hybrid teach.

| Score | What you see |
|---|---|
| 1 | The remote learner is never addressed. No question goes to the call, nobody checks that they can hear, and they learn about the pair task from what the microphone picks up |
| 2 | The remote learner is asked at the end of an activity, after the room has answered: "anything from the call?" The room votes by hand and the call in the poll, or the remote learner's help request waits until the room's are done |
| 3 | The same question, the same check and the same help queue for both: one poll that the room votes in too, a question to the call in its turn, help taken in the order of one queue |
| 4 | As 3, and the plan shows pacing per mode, such as a wait for the call or a poll left open until both have voted, and the facilitator changes something because the counts, the chat or the raised hands said so |

The inclusive part counts in the same domain. A visual that is shown and never described, a choice
made by colour alone, a handout only on the shared screen, and captions left off are each evidence
against 3, because the remote learner cannot get them in the same way as the room.

**Other domains, only when seen.** Conceptual questioning usually has a moment, the poll.
Prior knowledge has one if the facilitator says what the counts showed and takes a route from them.
Technical depth has one if a learner asks a question. Lab facilitation has one only if the slice
has hands-on work. Anything else is "not seen".

**Your one-to-one turn (about two minutes each).** In pod A's half 7, and in Phase 4 for pod B.

### Recovery moves

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| **Echo or howling in a pod room** | Every laptop in the room except the facilitator's (or the speakerphone) turns microphone and speaker off. Usually it is a laptop that rejoined with both on. |
| **A remote spot cannot reach the room page** (Phase 1, or the T-1 check) | The spot is on a different network. Find a spot on the same network. If there is none, the remote learner reads the page from your shared screen on the call, and you read it aloud as usual. Never move the remote spot into earshot to fix it. |
| **A remote learner cannot join the call within the first minute** | They swap with the in-room learner: the in-room learner takes their own laptop and headset to the remote spot and joins from there. The slot starts on time; the teach is scored as it runs. |
| **The poll will not open** | Everyone, the room included, types one letter in the chat. Same channel for everyone, so parity holds. Note it on the form's evidence. |
| **Breakout rooms will not open, or a co-host cannot open them** | The pair talks on the main call: the in-room learner puts on their headset and unmutes, and the facilitator mutes the room's microphone for the two minutes. |
| **Captions are not available** | Say so on the call. The facilitator types the key terms and the task into the chat. Note it in the capture list. |
| **A recording or a transcript starts** | Stop it at once and say so on the call. After the session, ask the call's owner to remove the file the same day, and note it in the capture list. |
| **A pod runs over into its changeover** | The timekeeper stops the teach at six minutes. The feedback round ends on the card's time, finished or not. The other pod's half never moves, and nor do you. |
| **You arrive late to a teach** | The teach starts on time without you. Write the minute you arrived on your form and score what you saw. If you saw less than four minutes, ask both peers what happened in front of the facilitator during the one-to-ones, and note it in the capture list. |
| **You are 3+ minutes late at 0:27** | Take the time from the share-out (down to one minute), then from the reflection. Do not shorten Phase 2 or any half. |
| **A participant's laptop cannot run the video tool** | See the T-7 note: a spare laptop, or a pod neighbour's at the remote spot. |
| **A participant is absent on the day** | Rebuild the pods from the group-size notes before 0:17, and move the forms to match. Do not run a pod of one. |
| **A participant has not done session 1** | They cannot take this session: there is no slice and no pod for them. They join the next session 1 and take session 4 afterwards. |
| **A participant arrives with no question for their poll** | They write one in Phase 2 with three options, and you check in your walk round that each wrong option is something a person could believe. The teach goes ahead with it. |
| **Someone asks whether the scores count** | No. They are formative, they are not observations, they never count toward clearance, and only the training function sees them (decision 0005). |

## AFTER

### Close protocol

- [ ] Count the forms again away from the room. Each should have an Instructor id, an Observer id,
      a score on remote and inclusive delivery, and a score or "not seen" on every other domain.
- [ ] Hand them to the coordinator the same day in a closed folder. They hold scores against
      instructor ids: do not photograph them and do not leave them in the room or at a remote spot.
- [ ] The coordinator scans each participant's forms into the private store under one key and
      records the session, with the date of the session:
      `teachermodel course record --instructor tm:instructor/<id> --session remote-delivery --evidence-ref <key> --on <YYYY-MM-DD>`
- [ ] The coordinator checks it with `teachermodel course status --instructor tm:instructor/<id>`:
      session 4 now shows a date instead of "missing".
- [ ] Check once more, the next day, that none of the three calls has a recording or a saved
      transcript in its history.
- [ ] Keep the paper until the head of training says what happens to it: `standards/retention.yaml`
      has no entry for training evidence yet.

### If a participant missed the session

They join the next group's session 4. There is no solo route: the session needs a room, a remote
learner and peers to score, and the course record takes a session only once it has happened.

### What participants take forward

- **Session 5** scores reference recordings on all five domains, remote and inclusive delivery
  among them.
- **Their real session.** Box 4.1 is the plan for its mode: where the question goes, the check they
  read from data, and the waits for the call.
